1 Overview

Geographically Weighted Summary Statistics (GWSS) compute local measures such as mean, standard deviation, or median. Nearby observations are given higher weights (via a kernel function and bandwidth), so each location reflects its own neighbourhood. This highlights spatial heterogeneity by revealing how statistical patterns vary across space.

##Application of GWSS

GWSS helps us see how relationships between data change in areas like the environment, health, and socio-economics. It can be used to study changes in ecosystems, find differences in health across regions, connect human activities to changes in the landscape, and spot unusual patterns in soil or minerals. Instead of giving one overall result, GWSS shows local patterns, making it easier to design policies and manage resources in a more focused way.

2 The Study Area and Data

The following two data sets are used here:

  1. Geospatial data: Hunan county boundary layer in shapefile format

  2. Aspatial data: Hunan_2012.csv for Hunan’s local development indicators in 2012.

4 Geographically Weighted Summary Statistics (GSWW) with adaptive bandwidth

4.1 Determine adaptive bandwidth

The code chunk below uses bw.gwr() for bandwith selection to calibrate a basic GWR model. - kernel could be given by gaussian, bisquare, exponential, tricube, and boxcar. - approach is specified by CV for cross-validation approach or by AIC corrected (AICc) approach. - adaptive is FALSE by default. Here we enforce it to compute an adaptive kernel.

bw_CV <- bw.gwr(GDPPC ~ 1,
                data = hunan_sp,
                approach = "CV",
                adaptive = TRUE,
                kernel = "bisquare",
                longlat = T) #if TRUE, great circle distances will be calculated

Based on GWSS, the correlation between GDPPC and GIO in Hunan is strong and largely linear, as indicated by higher local Pearson coefficients compared to Spearman’s rho. This suggests that increases in GIO are generally associated with increases in GDPPC across the province. Wealthier counties such as Changsha, as well as the western highlands (less suitable for agriculture and less developed), show relatively lower correlations, whereas the surrounding counties exhibit stronger correlations.
